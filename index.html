Maryland Homeowners: How to Check Your Property Records, Assessment, and Value

Most homeowners only think about property records when they buy, sell, or refinance. In between, the official record of your home keeps changing: assessments are updated, tax credits are applied or removed, and documents are recorded against your parcel. Checking your own record once in a while is one of the simplest ways to protect your investment, and Maryland property records are public and free to look up online.

Your property record affects real money. It determines your tax bill, shapes what buyers and lenders think your home is worth, and controls who the state believes owns your house. An error in the record, such as the wrong living area, a missing exemption, or an outdated owner name, can cost you money or delay a sale. Finding it early is far easier than fixing it under deadline.

This guide explains what Maryland homeowners should check in their own records, how the assessment cycle works, and what to do when something looks wrong.

Start with Your Parcel Card

The SDAT Real Property Data Search is the fastest way to see what the state has on file for your home. Search by your street address after selecting your county, and you will reach your parcel card. It shows your name and mailing address, the property address, the legal description, the map and parcel identifiers, the year the home was built, its living area, and the current assessed values for the land and the building.

Read the card the way a buyer or an appraiser would. Is the living area correct? Does the building description match your home? Is your mailing address current, so assessment notices actually reach you? Small mistakes in these fields are common, and they are worth correcting because they feed into your tax bill and your home's perceived value.

How Maryland Assessments Work

Maryland reassesses real property on a rolling cycle, with each property generally revalued once every three years. When your turn comes, SDAT sends a reassessment notice showing the new value. That notice is not a tax bill, but it is the number your future bills will be based on, so it deserves a careful read.

An assessment is the state's estimate of value for tax purposes, not a guarantee of market price. It is built from sales of comparable properties, the characteristics on your parcel card, and the condition of the local market. If your assessment jumps sharply, the explanation is often in those comparable sales, but it is also possible that the underlying data about your home is wrong, which is why checking the parcel card matters.

If you believe your assessment is incorrect, Maryland provides an appeal process. Owners typically begin by contacting their local SDAT assessment office to discuss the valuation, and formal appeal steps follow if the matter is not resolved. Keep your reassessment notice, gather evidence such as recent comparable sales, and do not ignore deadlines printed on the notice itself.

Tax Credits and Exemptions to Verify

Several Maryland programs can reduce a homeowner's tax bill, and the parcel card shows which ones are applied to your property. The best known is the Homestead Tax Credit, which limits how much the taxable assessment of an owner-occupied principal residence can rise from one year to the next. It is designed to shield long-term homeowners from sudden tax increases when values climb. The rate behind the bill matters too — check Maryland county property tax rates to see how your county compares with the rest of the state.

Other credits and exemptions exist for groups such as seniors, veterans, and people with disabilities, and the details vary by county. The important habit is to confirm that every credit you qualify for is actually showing on your record. Credits can lapse after a transfer, a refinancing, or a change in how the property is titled, and the county will not always catch the gap on its own.

Confirm Your Ownership and Title Details

Your deed is the document that proves you own your home, and it is recorded with the Clerk of the Circuit Court. You can view it through MDLandRec with a free account, using the liber and folio reference shown on your SDAT parcel card. It is worth reading your deed at least once: confirm the names are spelled correctly, confirm how title is held, and confirm that the legal description matches the property you believe you own.

Life events change title more often than people realize. A marriage, divorce, death in the family, or transfer into a trust should be followed by a recorded document. If the public record still shows a previous owner or an old form of ownership years later, a sale or a refinancing can stall while the paperwork is sorted out.

Before You Sell or Refinance

When a sale or refinance is on the horizon, a records check becomes urgent rather than optional. Buyers and lenders will examine the same records you can see, so reviewing them first lets you fix problems on your own schedule. Confirm that any old mortgage or lien from a paid-off loan has been released in the land records, because an unreleased lien is one of the most common causes of delayed closings.

Also review your permit history with the county. If you finished a basement, added a deck, or made other improvements, make sure the work appears in the county's records. Unpermitted work can reduce a buyer's offer or complicate an appraisal, and it is much easier to address before the house is listed.

What to Do When Something Is Wrong

  • Wrong facts on the parcel card: contact your local SDAT assessment office with documentation of the correct information.
  • Assessment you disagree with: discuss it with the assessment office first, then follow the appeal process described on your notice.
  • Missing tax credit: check the eligibility rules with SDAT or your county finance office and apply if you qualify.
  • Title or deed problem: speak with a Maryland real estate attorney before recording anything yourself.
  • Unreleased lien: contact the lender or lienholder and ask for a recorded release.

Conclusion

Your property record is the official version of your home's story, and it is worth reading from time to time. A quick check of your parcel card, your deed, and your credits can catch errors early, protect your tax bill, and keep a future sale or refinance on track. The tools are free, the search takes minutes, and the peace of mind is well worth the effort.
